# Scoring a job

Read before scoring. Without a rubric, scores drift generous, everything lands between 6 and 8,
and the number stops carrying information.

---

## What the score is for

**Deciding where to spend an evening.** That is the only purpose. A good application takes two
to four hours. The user has a limited number of those, and every hour spent on a role they
cannot get is an hour not spent on one they can.

So the score answers one question: *is this worth their time?* It does not measure how much they
would enjoy the job, how much they want it, or how good they are. It is not a verdict on them
and it must never read as one.

**Be blunt.** A generous score is not a kindness. It costs them an evening and produces the
silence that later makes their own results look worse than they are.

---

## The bands

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **9-10** | Meets everything essential and most desirables. They would be a credible frontrunner. Rare, and if you are giving these out often, you are being generous. |
| **7-8** | Meets everything essential. Real gaps exist but are the kind a good application can address. Worth applying. |
| **5-6** | Meets most essentials, missing something that matters. Worth applying only if they want it enough to write a genuinely strong case, or if the market is thin. |
| **3-4** | Missing something important, or the match is superficial. Usually not worth the evening. |
| **1-2** | Not eligible, wrong field, or a keyword coincidence. |

Most jobs are 4 to 7. If a batch comes out mostly 8s, recalibrate: you are matching on
vocabulary rather than on what the employer actually needs.

---

## Blockers come first

Before scoring, check for a requirement that makes the application futile no matter how good the
candidate is. Set `blocker: true` and score 1 or 2.

Common ones, and they matter far more outside software than inside it:

- **A licence, registration or qualification that is legally required.** Nursing and midwifery
  registration, QTS, a practising certificate, chartered status, a licence to practise. In these
  fields it is not a preference and no cover letter overcomes it.
- **A specific degree** where the field genuinely requires one.
- **Security clearance** they do not hold and the employer will not sponsor.
- **Right to work** they do not have where the employer says they will not sponsor.
- **A location** they cannot reach, for a role stated as fully on-site.
- **Years of experience** stated as a hard minimum and missed by a wide margin. Missing by one
  year is not a blocker; missing by six is.

Say which requirement blocks it, quoting the advert's own wording. "They ask for current NMC
registration" is actionable. "Not a great fit" is not.

Do not invent blockers from soft language. "Ideally you will have" is a desirable, not a bar.

---

## Then judge the substance

Read what the employer actually needs done, not the words they used. Two adverts using entirely
different vocabulary can describe the same job, and two using identical vocabulary can describe
different ones.

For each essential requirement, find the evidence in the profile. An achievement that
demonstrates the requirement counts. A tag that merely mentions it does not.

**Weight by what the advert emphasises.** What appears in the first paragraph, or is repeated,
is what they care about. Requirements listed ninth of eleven are usually aspirational.

**Recency matters.** Something done last year is worth more than the same thing done in 2016,
and in fast-moving fields much more.

**Scale matters.** Running a team of three is not evidence for running a department of forty.
Say so rather than letting it pass.

---

## What not to score on

**Keyword overlap.** The most common failure. A profile and an advert sharing eight nouns does
not mean the person can do the job.

**Job title.** Titles are not standardised. A "manager" at one organisation does the work of a
"lead" at another and a "director" at a third. Read the responsibilities.

**How much the user wants it.** Not your input, and not something the employer weighs.

**Seniority inflation in either direction.** A role a level above them is an `adjacent` fit with
a real gap, not a 9 because it sounds impressive. A role a level below is not a 9 either; it is
a poor use of their time and often a rejection on overqualification.

**Your own enthusiasm.** If a role sounds interesting to you, that is not evidence.

---

## Fit type

Relative to *this person's* current work, not to any industry norm.

- **`core`** — the job they already do, in the same field, at roughly the same level
- **`adjacent`** — a step up, or a sideways move into neighbouring work that reuses most of what
  they have
- **`pivot`** — a change of field, where most of the case rests on transferable capability

Fit type is independent of score. A `pivot` can be an 8 when the employer explicitly wants
outside experience. A `core` role can be a 3 when they miss an essential.

Fit type drives how `/apply` frames the CV, so getting it right matters beyond triage.

---

## The rationale

Two or three sentences. It must contain:

1. **The specific match**, naming actual evidence rather than a category. Not "strong relevant
   experience" but "ten years running referral services, which is the whole job here".
2. **The most important gap**, in the employer's own terms.
3. **What that gap means for the application**, where it is not obvious.

Write it as you would say it to a friend who asked whether to bother. Direct, specific, no
padding, no encouragement.

Bad: *"This is a strong match for your background with some minor gaps around qualifications."*

Good: *"Ten years running referral services is exactly the job. They list a professional
qualification as essential and you do not have it, so this only works if you can show the
experience substitutes, and some employers will not budge on that."*

## Learnings

Read `my/learnings.md`. Where an observation genuinely applies, let it move the score, and say so
in the rationale so the influence is visible and can be argued with.

Do not stretch an observation to fit. Two data points is not a pattern, and the file will say so
itself when there is not enough evidence yet.
