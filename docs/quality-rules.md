# Quality Rules

Craft standards applied to every generated document, for every user, in every field.

These are not matters of taste. Personal taste lives in `my/voice.md`, which every user writes
for themselves. **Where a Voice Profile conflicts with a rule here, the rule here wins.**

Any command that produces a document the user will send applies this file.

---

## 1. Truthfulness

**Every claim must trace to Provenance.** Nothing appears in a generated document unless it
traces to something the user said or a document they supplied. There is no exception for
phrasing that sounds plausible.

**Do not strengthen a claim in the rewriting.** Watch for drift in the verb: *contributed to*
becoming *led*, *helped with* becoming *owned*, *was part of a team that* becoming *delivered*.
This drift is easy to introduce and hard to spot on a reread, and it is the difference between a
CV that survives an interview and one that does not.

**When the strongest supportable phrasing is unclear, ask.** Show the user both versions and let
them choose. If they confirm the stronger one, update the Profile so the question is settled
once rather than asked every time.

**Never invent detail about an employer.** Anything specific about the organisation must come
from the advert or from something the user has told you. If you have nothing genuine, leave a
visible placeholder. A plausible-sounding invention is worse than an obvious gap, because the
user will not catch it.

**Numbers are either real or absent.** Do not estimate, round up, or infer a metric that was
never stated.

---

## 2. The stranger test

After drafting, reread the whole document as someone who has never met this person and knows
nothing about their employers, their industry's internal language, or their projects. The reader
knows only what is on the page.

**Cut or explain every internal name.** Project code names, internal team names, product names
that are not public, and employer-specific acronyms mean nothing to a reader and make them feel
excluded. Replace with a plain description of the thing.

**Distinguish industry acronyms from internal ones.** Terms every practitioner in the target
field knows are fine and cutting them reads as naive. Terms only that employer used are noise.
When unsure, expand on first use.

**Cut detail that is specific without being informative.** Internal reference numbers, document
names, tool version numbers, granular hour counts, and counts too small to impress. Keep detail
where the detail is itself the point.

**Cut parentheticals a reader cannot evaluate.** If a reader cannot do anything with the
information in the brackets, the brackets are noise.

---

## 3. Language

**Active voice.** Rewrite anything of the form *was managed by*, *was responsible for*,
*was tasked with*. The person did things; say so.

**Lead with the doing, not the having.** *Responsible for a team of six* describes a job
description. *Built a team of six* describes a person.

**Consistent tense and person within a section.** Do not mix implied-subject bullets with
first-person bullets in the same list. The Voice Profile decides which style is used; this rule
only requires that the choice is applied consistently.

**No filler.** *Successfully*, *various*, *a range of*, *helped to*, *worked closely with* used
as padding, and any sentence that would read identically on anyone else's CV.

---

## 4. Honesty about roles

**Describe what was actually done, not the most flattering available title for it.** If the
contribution to a community organisation was building their website, say that. Do not describe
it as leading their operations. This applies with particular force to volunteering, side
projects, and informal responsibilities, where inflation is common and unnecessary. The real
version is usually more interesting.

**Do not imply seniority that was not held.** Leading a project is not managing a team. Being
the most experienced person present is not being in charge.

---

## 5. Formatting for the destination

**The DOCX version is for machines.** Single column, standard heading names that a parser
recognises (Experience, Education, Skills), no tables, no text boxes, no images, no icons, no
columns, no headers or footers carrying content. Anything clever here loses information for the
candidate.

**The PDF version is for humans.** Design freedom, within the page target set in the user's
configuration.

**Respect the page target.** It is set in configuration because the right answer differs by
country and field: two pages is standard for UK industry, one page is a hard convention for US
résumés, and an academic CV can run to many pages with a full publication list. Overrunning a
convention the reader holds is a real cost.
